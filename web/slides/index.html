Water gets checked rarely, and the result doesn't say what to do

  • Stony Brook runs through Princeton. Since 2000 it has been sampled on 17 days, none since 2023.
  • Places with weak infrastructure or no cell coverage get checked least.
  • Even with a sample, a number on a report doesn't tell you what to do next.

One cup, one loop

  1. Measure

    Pour a cup. TapTest reads pH, dissolved solids (TDS) and clarity.

  2. Interpret

    Claude reads the numbers and the site's history, and explains them in plain language.

  3. Act

    The cup tips itself into the right filter: ZeroWater for dissolved solids, Brita for cloudiness.

  4. Compare

    Pour the filtered water back. The retest shows whether it worked.

AI that explains, inside guardrails

ZeroWater

Tipped into ZeroWater by Claude

TDS is 782 ppm, above the 300 ppm comfort level and over the EPA's 500 ppm guideline. ZeroWater strips dissolved solids, so the cup goes there. Pour it back afterwards to confirm.

  • Claude picks the filter and says why. If it hasn't answered within 6 seconds, the tank uses its own thresholds, so the cup always moves.
  • After every retest, a LangChain deep agent checks the site's history, nearby nodes, filter life and published data, then writes findings and next steps.
  • It never claims to find lead, bacteria or specific contaminants. Our sensors can't, so neither can it.

Live demo

  1. Pour salty water. TDS reads high and Claude sends it to ZeroWater.
  2. Pour it back. The retest drops to a few ppm, and the filter count goes up.
  3. Pour tea. It reads cloudy and goes to Brita instead.
